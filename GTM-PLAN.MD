# GTM-PLAN — LivingBook

> Draft v0.1 — will be refined as sponsor research completes and founder questions are
> answered.

## Positioning

**"Books that talk back."** LivingBook is the first platform where a real author's book
becomes a living video agent that answers readers **in the author's own words** — never
inventing, always quoting — rendered as shareable talking-head video by Masky.

- For **authors**: a new owned channel (embed on your site), a new revenue stream (share
  of reader credit spend), and a marketing agent that works trending moments 24/7.
- For **readers**: ask any book anything, get the exact passage — spoken to your face by
  the narrator or the characters.
- Wedge: **the Bible, free, in the Public Domain shelf** — instant content, massive
  existing question-volume ("what does the Bible say about…"), zero author acquisition
  needed on day one.

### Stage 1 — Public-domain led growth
- Launch the landing page with 3–5 public-domain "living books" (Bible first; then e.g.
  Meditations, Art of War, Pride & Prejudice — high question-volume classics).
- **Newsjacking engine as content marketing:** the marketing agent publishes 1–3
  topical clips/day ("what does {book} say about {trending topic}") to X/TikTok/
  YouTube Shorts. Every clip = agent demo + share link. This is the self-evolving agent
  eating its own dog food, publicly.
- SEO: each Q&A becomes a shareable page ("What does the Bible say about X") targeting
  the enormous existing search volume for that exact phrase pattern.

### Stage 2 — Author acquisition (supply side)
- Target first: **non-fiction authors with existing audiences and podcast back-catalogs**
  (self-help, business, theology) — their transcript corpus makes the agent instantly
  good, and they already sell direct.
- Pitch: "Your book answers DMs now." Embed widget on their site in one script tag;
  revenue share on reader credit spend; their podcast archive becomes an asset again.
- Channels: podcast guesting circuit, author newsletters, indie-publishing communities
  (Kindlepreneur, 20BooksTo50k), literary agents for mid-list backlist deals.
- White-glove onboard the first 10 authors personally; their share clips recruit the
  next 100.

### Stage 3 — Reader app (demand side)
- App Store launch of the React Native app once ≥20 living books exist (public domain +
  early authors). ASO around "talk to books", "ask the Bible".
- Social loop: every answer clip is one-tap reshareable with the book's link; Keeper/
  Circle mechanics (see REALWORLDPLAN.MD) drive retention and local virality.

## Revenue model

1. **Credit spend share** — readers spend Masky credits on voice/video answers; the
   author earns a portion; LivingBook takes a platform cut. (Exact splits TBD — open
   question to founder; depends on whether Masky provides a revenue-share ledger.)
2. **Creator economy (Podcast tab)** — *"some of the best ideas in the world come from
   the most unexpected places"*: any user who co-hosts a viral episode becomes a
   creator. Fans subscribe to their future podcasts, pay to feature the creator's
   avatar on their own episodes (generated), or book **real recorded video-to-video
   sessions** published on the platform. Credit-split between creator, featured
   authors, and platform.
3. **Author subscriptions** (later) — pro tools: analytics, marketing-agent autopilot,
   multiple avatars/characters per book, priority rendering.
4. **Sponsored landmarks / events** (later, see REALWORLDPLAN.MD) — bookstores,
   churches, publishers.

## Metrics that matter

- Hackathon window: demo clips shared, judges' rubric coverage, sponsor tools visibly used.
- Stage 1: clips published/day, share-through rate, question volume per book.
- Stage 2: authors onboarded, corpus units ingested per author, author-embed installs.
- Stage 3: DAU, favorites per user, credit spend per MAU, author payout totals.

## Risks / dependencies

- Masky credit economics must leave room for a meaningful author share.
- Copyright: only author-authorized uploads + true public domain; verbatim-quote design
  is also a legal asset (no derivative-text hallucination).
- Trending-signal API costs/access (X API pricing) for the marketing agent.
- App Store review friction for user-generated/religious content — mitigation: strict
  verbatim sourcing and author verification.
